# Research summary: 3D printed Lincoln Logs

Gathered with Tavily searches in November 2026. The extraction of four model pages (Printables, MakerWorld, Make.toys, Reddit) failed, so model details come from search snippets only.

## Existing 3D models

- **Free:** StrandedOrg's [complete set](https://www.printables.com/model/484036-lincoln-logs-complete-set), also on [MakerWorld](https://makerworld.com/en/models/1046438-lincoln-logs-complete-set); Chipped Builds' [Lincoln Logs](https://makerworld.com/en/models/101495-lincoln-logs); a [mini desktop set](https://www.make.toys/toys/desktop_mini_lincoln_logs_0); Unclebooch's [door](https://www.printables.com/model/1069857-lincoln-logs-door); [G-scale doors and windows](https://www.facebook.com/groups/452359386658083/posts/1057319359495413).
- **Paid or "inspired":** a [Cults3D cabin](https://cults3d.com/:4122929) at US$4.99; a [Creality Cloud playset](https://www.crealitycloud.com/model-detail/lincoln-log-inspired-large-log-cabin). More are indexed on [Yeggi](https://www.yeggi.com/q/lincoln+log). Etsy sells ready-made prints.

## Original dimensions

- Log diameter 3/4 in; 3/4 in square notches; 1-, 2-, 3- and 4-notch logs ([Log Cabin Hub](https://logcabinhub.com/lincoln-logs), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Logs)).
- The "2 1/4 in" spacing is the gap between notches; centres are 3 in apart ([ibuildit.ca](https://ibuildit.ca/projects/lincoln-logs)).
- Hobbyist plans claiming compatibility cut notches 3/16 in deep on both faces and leave a 3/8 in shoulder at each end (ibuildit.ca). They use a log rip of 11/16 in so a log has 1/16 in play in a 3/4 in seat.
- A vintage original 2-notch log is listed at 4-5/8 in ([eBay](https://www.ebay.com.sg/b/Lincoln-Logs-Building-Toys/183446/bn_1929286?Color=Brown&_udlo=19&mag=1&rt=nc)), which implies a 7/16 in end shoulder.
- A different homemade design uses 4 in spacing and 5.5 / 10 / 15 in logs ([WoodCentral](https://www.woodcentral.com/articles/lincoln_logs.php)); that is a larger scale, not the original.
- A seller's [Poshmark listing](https://poshmark.com/listing/Lincoln-Log-Pieces-697ff0d8e9c77d6090234e27) of a mixed lot has 11 photos, including an inventory sheet. Lengths in inches: 1-insert log 1 5/8, 2-insert 4 1/2 and 6, 3-insert 7 3/4, 4-insert 10 5/8, half logs 4 1/2 and 7 1/2, triangle gable 4 x 2, beveled ends 1 1/2 x 1 1/4 and 4 3/4, plastic gable 2 1/4 x 8 1/2, shingles (slats) 6, 9 and 11 plus ridge shingles 5 5/8 to 9. The 1- and 4-insert lengths match a 3 in notch centre and 7/16 in shoulders exactly.
- Measured from the photos with a pixel grid (scale from the mat's 1 in grid and the 3 in notch spacing):
  - **Logs:** 3/4 in diameter with 3/4 in notches on 3 in centres; ends 0.33 to 0.44 in.
  - **Half logs:** flat-bottomed, about 0.53 in tall, notches on top.
  - **"Hex" 1-insert:** faceted end with flats top and bottom.
  - **Beveled End 4 3/4:** a 2-insert log with rounded ends; the small beveled pieces are cut at about 39 deg.
  - **Wooden triangle:** about 4 7/8 x 2 in, straight 39 deg slopes, two bottom notches 3 in apart.
  - **Plastic gable:** symmetric, 3 bottom notches 3 in apart, about 7 steps per side, eave lips.
  - **Shingles:** flat shingles about 0.8 in wide; ridge shingles about 1 1/8 in wide with a centre groove.
  - Full table in `DESIGN_NOTES.md`.
- Roof pieces in sets: a 371-piece set lists "6 roof eaves, 28 roof slats" ([TTPM review](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NlhMvjbtn2k)); some 1970s sets had clips to hold slats together ([Architoys](https://www.architoys.net/toys/toypages/linclogs.html)); a roof-support model on [Printables](https://www.printables.com/model/659905-lincoln-logs-roof-supports-84-variations-different) says the original lip design lets slats fall off.
- No exact original dimensions found for doors, windows or the chimney.

## Printing tolerances (general FDM guidance)

- PLA sliding fit 0.15 to 0.25 mm per side; PETG 0.2 to 0.3 mm ([Sovol](https://www.sovol3d.com/blogs/news/fdm-3d-printing-tolerances-clearances-how-to-design-parts-that-fit)).
- UF Libraries: standard sliding 0.20 to 0.25 mm, snug 0.15 to 0.20 mm ([UF Makerspace](https://makerspace.uflib.ufl.edu/services/3dprocess/recommended-software-for-3d-printing/designing-for-3d-printing-tolerances-on-the-mk4-and-xl)).
- Prusa forum user: 0.1 to 0.2 mm held but forgiving in PLA ([Prusa forum](https://forum.prusa3d.com/forum/english-forum-general-discussion-announcements-and-releases/designing-and-printing-interlocking-parts-first-time-advice-please)).
- None of these tested logs specifically.

## History and legal

- Invented about 1916 by John Lloyd Wright; patent issued 1920 ([Richfield Historical Society](https://www.richfieldhistoricalsociety.org/story_lincoln_logs.html)). Now made by Basic Fun under Hasbro's IP; Basic Fun filed for Chapter 11 in 2024 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Logs)).
- "LINCOLN LOGS" is a registered Hasbro trademark for building blocks ([Justia](https://trademarks.justia.com/715/38/lincoln-logs-71538955.html)). The patent has expired, so the geometry is free to copy but the name is not. I found no case law on shared 3D files.
- Store sets cost roughly $30 (59 pieces) to $110 (327 pieces) (Log Cabin Hub).
